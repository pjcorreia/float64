# Crossing the estuary from Oeiras — scenario test

**Question:** would it be possible to keep a La Mare Apartboat XL at the Oeiras marina and, on a weekend, cross the Tagus estuary and anchor it near the opposite bank?

**Short answer:** the three elements of the scenario (Apartboat XL + Oeiras home berth + weekend crossings) are valid two at a time, but the three together don't coexist. This is a requirements conflict, not a matter of willpower.

## Facts

### Entering and berthing at Oeiras: yes, it fits

- Porto de Recreio de Oeiras limits: **25 m length, 6 m beam, 3 m draft**. A 12×5 m Apartboat XL with ~0.5 m draft clears with room to spare.
- A berth for 5 m of beam is a direct conversation with the marina — expect a **multihull surcharge**.
- The problem isn't the pontoon, it's the water outside: the port sits at the mouth of the Tagus, with prevailing N/NW winds (the *nortada*). Oeiras is a boat marina facing the Atlantic, with real sea at its doorstep.

### The crossing is where the scenario breaks

- From Oeiras, "the opposite bank" is Trafaria/Costa da Caparica — and what lies in between is the **throat of the estuary**: the main shipping channel with commercial traffic, tidal currents that easily exceed 2–3 knots on spring tides, and Atlantic swell rolling in through the bar.
- A CE **category D** boat is certified for waves up to **0.3 m**; on that stretch, that exists for a handful of glassy dawns per month in summer.
- A 12-metre box with walls that act as sails, two small outboards and a **5-knot** top speed has zero margin when the wind shifts at 11 a.m.
- Outside its design category, **insurance** also lands in swampy territory.
- Even delivering the boat to Oeiras by water would need a weather window; by road, 5 m of beam means an **abnormal load permit**.

**Verdict:** with an Apartboat XL, that specific crossing is technically possible in a few hand-picked windows and imprudent in all the others.

## The same dream in the right geography

Base at **Parque das Nações** and the same weekend becomes a viable classic:

- Leave on a calm morning with the tide in your favour, cross the **Mar da Palha** — the inner half of the estuary: wide, sheltered, no bar.
- Anchor in **Seixal bay** (mud, excellent holding, protected all around) or on the **Alcochete/Montijo** flats — 1h–1h30 from home at 5 knots.
- Wake up at anchor in front of the flamingos; return with the tide.
- Oeiras becomes 20 minutes by train instead of 20 minutes by boat.

## Requirements generated by this scenario (→ Phase 1.3 matrix)

- **Oversized anchor** for a houseboat's windage (sail-walls).
- **Anchor light**.
- Tides studied before every departure — the Alcochete/Montijo flats **dry out**.
- **Daytime return** — the Marinheiro licence is daylight-only navigation.
- **Dinghy** — Marley needs to get ashore, and nobody rows a house.
